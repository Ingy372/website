Our Story

Changing our world, one black belt at a time.

Michael Lowery stepped onto a mat at eight years old and never really stepped off. Karate. Jiu Jitsu. Kung Fu. Tae Kwon Do. The five traditional Japanese weapons. A black belt in each one.

Somewhere along the line he saw a way to glorify God through martial arts. So he built a system that put the two together, and he named it after the reason: Yeshua's Ryu. Jesus' Way.

He spent three decades on it. Refining the system, teaching the classes, watching students walk in as one person and leave as another. It became his life's work. To see people grow in Christ, and to send them into the world able to protect themselves and the people they love.

Then he handed it to his stepson.

Jayson Lindemuth runs the school today. Every class. Every student. Same system. Same building. Same conviction that a punch should be taught beside a principle, and that a student is trained in mind, body, and spirit or they are not really being trained at all.

We keep no trophy case. We are not raising competitors. We are raising the kind of people you would want living next door.

"A Black Belt is not something you simply earn. A Black Belt is who you are becoming."

Questions & Answers

Everything you were going to ask anyway.

Thirty-three years of parents standing in our lobby, asking good questions. Here are the answers, written the way we would give them to you in person.

Getting Started About Your Child Your First Class Our School Visiting Us

Getting Started

Ages & Timing
What age can a child start martial arts?

Children can begin at age three in our Little Warriors program. Ages six through twelve train in our Kids classes. Teens and adults train together from thirteen up.

Is three too young to start martial arts?

No. Little Warriors was designed specifically for ages three to five. It is our program for our littlest students, and it was built for them from the ground up.

At that age we are teaching a child to listen. To wait their turn. To try something hard and not quit when it gets hard. The kicks and blocks are the delivery method. The lesson is underneath.

Is my child too old to start? Is it too late to start as an adult?

No, and no.

We meet our students exactly where they are the day they walk in. Your level of knowledge, your fitness, your experience: none of it matters to us. We treat our students like members of our family, and we build you from the ground up.

Can I train alongside my child?

Yes, once they reach age twelve.

Our teen and adult class is where parents and older children train side by side. We also hold Saturday classes, where the entire dojo trains together regardless of age or rank.

About Your Child

The Real Questions
Will martial arts make my child aggressive?

In our experience it does the opposite, and it is something we take seriously.

We spend as much time on what martial arts is not as on what it is. We talk about anger. What it feels like, what to do with it, and where these skills belong and where they never do. A student who cannot control themselves has not learned martial arts. They have only learned motion.

We are also a non-competitive school. We keep no trophy case. We are not raising fighters. We are raising the kind of people you would want living next door.

Does martial arts help with ADHD, focus, or behavior at school?

We are instructors, not clinicians, so we are not going to promise you an outcome. What we can tell you is what we have seen in this building.

Students with ADHD have trained here and progressed. Students with autism have trained here and progressed. One nonverbal student advanced a good way through our system.

We think the structure is part of it. A class has clear expectations, one task at a time, an instructor watching, and a belt you can see coming. That is a good environment for a lot of children, and it is a good environment for children who struggle to find one.

Bring your child in for a free week. You will learn more from watching one class than from anything we could write here.

Does martial arts help with bullying?

Yes, though often in ways parents do not expect.

We never encourage a student to fight, and we never encourage them to become the aggressor. We do teach them to stand up for themselves, including when that is difficult.

Here is what we watch happen. A student grows physically capable. They accomplish things they did not believe they could. Their confidence rises with it. And a confident child handles confrontation differently and holds a boundary more firmly, often without ever needing to raise a hand.

My child is shy or has special needs. Will this work?

Come and let us meet them.

We have taught quiet children, anxious children, children who could not be still, children with physical disabilities, and children who did not speak. Progress looks different for every one of them, and we are not going to pretend otherwise.

The free week costs you nothing. It will tell you more than any answer we could write here.

Is it safe? Do students hit each other?

Safety comes before everything else in this building.

Students work at a level appropriate to their age and rank, always supervised. We teach control long before we teach contact, because a student who has not learned control has no business making any.

Your First Class

Practical Details
What should my child wear to their first class?

Comfortable clothes they can move in. A t-shirt and athletic shorts or sweatpants is perfect. We train barefoot, so no shoes are needed.

Please do not buy a uniform before your free week. If you enroll, we will fit them properly.

How long is a class?

Little Warriors classes run forty-five minutes. Our kids, teen, and adult classes run a full hour.

What if we go on vacation or miss classes?

We keep track of the days your student misses and offer them back as make-ups.

Come in on one of your off days and pick up an additional session until you are caught up. Saturday classes are also open to everyone, both as an extra day of training and as a chance to make up anything missed.

Our School

What We Teach
What is Yeshua's Ryu?

It is our system. Our founder, Michael Lowery, built it.

The name means Jesus' Way. It draws on Karate, Jiu Jitsu, Kung Fu, Tae Kwon Do, and the five traditional Japanese weapons, taught alongside Christian character.

Do we have to be Christian to train here?

No.

Our faith shapes how we teach character, and we will not hide that. The name of the system says it plainly. But we ask nothing of what our students believe, and we never have. Families of every background have trained on this mat and been welcome on it.

What is the difference between karate, jiu jitsu, and taekwondo?

Broadly speaking: karate emphasizes strikes. Jiu jitsu emphasizes grappling and ground control. Taekwondo emphasizes kicking.

Most schools teach one of them. We teach a mixed traditional system drawing from all three, plus Kung Fu and traditional weapons.

Do you offer birthday parties or a parents night out?

Both.

Our facility has an indoor ninja line, a throwing wall for ninja stars, knives, and blow darts, an outdoor playground, and a trampoline.

And at every birthday party, the birthday child cuts the cake with a real Chinese double-edged straight sword.

Small students. Big beginnings.

Little Warriors is martial arts built from the ground up for our youngest students. Before the kicks and blocks, they learn to listen, to focus, and to finish what they start.

A three, four, or five year old is not short on energy, curiosity, or opinions. What they are still learning is how to focus it. How to wait their turn. How to follow a direction the first time it is given, and how to keep going when something gets hard.

That is what Little Warriors is really for. The martial arts are real, but at this age they are also the delivery method for something bigger: the focus and self-control that will follow your child into kindergarten and every classroom after it.

Built by hand, for a purpose.

Our founder built this 2,400 square foot dojo himself, on two cleared acres. Every wall, every mat, and every inch of space was placed with intention, shaped around what a martial arts school is meant to be.

That care shows up everywhere you look. Inside and out, we have room for the things that turn training into something a student genuinely looks forward to.

Ninja line photo
Inside the Dojo

The Ninja Line

Stretched across the heart of the dojo, our ninja line is a hanging obstacle course of grips, holds, and hand-over-hand challenges that put every muscle in the upper body to work.

Students conquer it one hold at a time, week after week, building grip strength, balance, and body control while they are having far too much fun to call it exercise.

Throwing wall photo
Inside the Dojo

The Throwing Wall

Ninja stars. Knives. Blow darts. Our dedicated throwing wall is a purpose-built target area where students get to take aim and let fly, always under close supervision.

It takes focus, a steady hand, and control, and there is nothing quite like the thrill of a throw that lands exactly where you meant it to. It is a favorite the moment students earn their turn at it.

Playground photo
Outside

The Outdoor Playground

Out on our two acres, we have a full outdoor playground. It is where siblings play during class, and where everybody heads once training wraps up.

It is a small thing that makes a big difference, especially for a parent trying to keep one kid entertained while another trains.

Trampoline photo
Outside

The Trampoline

Right alongside the playground sits a trampoline, one more spot for kids to burn off energy and have a little fun before or after class.

Bringing the whole family? We planned for that.

Between the ninja line inside and the playground and trampoline outside, there is somewhere for everyone to be. A sibling who is not in class has plenty of room to play, so the whole family can come along and feel at home.

Our Story

Thirty-three years, one standard.

Action Zone was not built to be the biggest school in the area. It was built to change who walks out the door.

In 1993, Michael Lowery built a martial arts school next to his own home in Raeford. He is still teaching in it.

What he built was not a franchise or a fitness trend. Yeshua's Ryu is his own system, drawn from more than fifty years in the art, blending Karate, Jiu Jitsu, Kung Fu, Tae Kwon Do, and the five traditional Japanese weapons into one thing that gets taught the same way every night.

We are a non-competitive school. That is on purpose, and Michael has always been direct about why.

Michael Lowery portrait

Our focus isn't on winning trophies or gaining recognition. Instead, we seek self-enlightenment and a deeper spiritual understanding through the demanding practice of martial arts.

Knowledge is incomplete without wisdom, and our habits define who we become.

That standard shows up in who is allowed to teach here. Every instructor has put in a minimum of 480 hours to earn their Black Belt, plus a leadership program on top of it. Your child is not being handed off to someone who learned the moves last year.

The school is run today by Michael's stepson, Jayson Lindemuth, who first stepped onto this mat at three years old and has spent twenty-six years in the art since. Same building. Same system. Same standard.

Thirty-three years in one town means most of the people on this floor are not strangers, and we teach like it.

A Black Belt is not something you simply earn, though you will.
